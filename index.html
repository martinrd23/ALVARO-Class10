1 · Multiword verbs: how they work

phrasal verbs · meaning in context

Multiword verbs combine a verb with one or more particles.

VerbMeaningExample
find outdiscoverWe need to find out what happened.
carry outperform / conductThey carried out an inspection.
get back toreply laterI'll get back to you tomorrow.
look intoinvestigateThe company is looking into the issue.
Common trap: Do not translate word by word. The complete expression carries the meaning.
Exam strategy: Replace the multiword verb with a one-word synonym and check whether the sentence still makes sense.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We need to ___ what caused the problem.
2. The team will ___ the inspection tomorrow.
3. I'll ___ you tomorrow with the answer.

2 · Transitive, intransitive, separable & inseparable

object placement · particle behavior

Some multiword verbs take an object; others do not.

TypeExampleKey point
IntransitiveThe machine broke down.No object.
Transitive separableTurn off the light / Turn the light off.Object can move.
PronounTurn it off.Pronoun goes in the middle.
InseparableLook after the equipment.Object stays after particle.
Common trap: Turn off it ✗ → Turn it off.
Exam strategy: If a pronoun appears, check whether the phrasal verb is separable.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. Please turn ___ the lights.
2. Which is correct?
3. The generator broke ___.

work · communication · change · effort

Learn these as chunks, not isolated translations.

VerbMeaningExample

Quick Check · 3 items

4 · Collocations & compound nouns

natural combinations · vocabulary precision

Vocabulary questions often test natural combinations.

PatternExamples
verb + nounmake a decision · take a risk · meet a deadline · solve a problem
adjective + nounheavy traffic · strong evidence · serious problem · practical solution
noun + nounjob interview · safety equipment · customer service · traffic jam
Common trap: A combination can be grammatically possible but still not be the expected collocation.
Exam strategy: Read the words around the blank and choose the standard combination.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. We need to ___ a decision today.
2. There was ___ traffic.
3. Which is a common compound noun?

7 · Functional language: identify the intention

opinions · requests · suggestions · complaints

Several options may be grammatical, but only one matches the speaker's purpose.

FunctionUseful language
OpinionIn my opinion... · I think...
SuggestionWhy don't we...? · We could...
RequestCould you...? · Would you mind...?
ApologyI'm sorry about... · I apologize for...
ComplaintI'm afraid there's a problem with...
DisagreementI see your point, but...
Exam strategy: Identify the communicative function before choosing the phrase.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. ___ you help me, please?
2. Why don't we ___ a taxi?
3. I'm afraid there's a problem with my room. This is a ___.

8 · Reading: main idea, detail & reference

read for purpose · locate evidence

Different questions require different reading strategies.

Question typeStrategy
Main ideaFocus on repeated ideas and overall purpose.
Specific detailLocate the exact relevant sentence.
ReferenceLook backward to identify what a pronoun refers to.
PurposeAsk why the writer included the text or paragraph.

Mini text

A local company introduced flexible schedules last year. Employees can now start earlier or later depending on their responsibilities. This has improved attendance and reduced stress.

Exam strategy: Replace a reference word with the possible noun or idea and check whether the sentence still makes sense.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. In the mini text, "This" refers to ___.
2. What result is mentioned?
3. The text is mainly about ___.

9 · Reading: inference, context clues & distractors

what is implied · eliminate traps

Inference means reaching a reasonable conclusion from evidence.

Mini text

Daniel arrived at the station at 7:55. His train was scheduled to leave at 8:00, but the platform was crowded and the doors closed before he reached them.

Reasonable inference: Daniel probably missed the train.

Common distractors

  • True in general but unsupported.
  • Uses words from the text but changes the meaning.
  • Too extreme.
  • Answers a different question.
Exam strategy: Choose the option with the strongest textual support.

Quick Check · 3 items

New mini text for the Quick Check

Maya had an important presentation at 9:00 a.m. She left home early, but there was an accident on the highway. At 8:50, she was still several kilometers away from the office.

1. What can we infer about Maya?
2. A distractor can ___.
3. For inference, choose the answer ___.

10 · Timed reading & exam integration

pace · elimination · cumulative grammar

Reading is part of the same 60-minute exam.

1 · Read the questionKnow what information you need.
2 · ScanLocate the relevant part.
3 · EliminateRemove contradicted or unsupported options.
4 · Move onDo not overinvest in one item.

Cumulative grammar still matters.

Reading and vocabulary items can contain grammar from Sessions 1–9. Tense, pronouns, modals, conditionals, verb patterns, relatives, passive and reported speech can all affect meaning.

Common trap: You are already much faster than the official average. Accuracy should take priority over more speed.
Exam strategy: Use your time reserve to verify the full sentence or paragraph when two options look possible.

Quick Check · 3 items

1. For a detail question, first ___.
2. If two answers seem possible, choose the one ___.
3. You should now focus more on ___.
Week 10 exam habit: Use context before translation. For vocabulary, identify the field and collocation. For reading, identify the question type and locate evidence before choosing.
